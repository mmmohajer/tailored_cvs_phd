# Cover letter structure

## Findings

- Tailored content is what earned the callback lift; generic letters barely moved it (12.5% vs 10.7%). [S03, C]
- Hiring professionals in the same survey named a generic letter as one of the most common things that hurts an applicant. [S03, C]
- Short declarative statements beat paragraphs for skimmers; the same reading behavior applies to letters. [S01, C]

There is no controlled study of letter structure. This four-part shape is grade D and exists to force tailoring, which is the part with evidence behind it.

## What the agent does

**Header:** same three lines as the resume. Then date, recipient name (or "Hiring Team"), company.

**Salutation:** a named person when the posting or the person provides one; otherwise "Dear Hiring Team." Never "To whom it may concern."

**Body (four short paragraphs, 250–400 words total)**

1. **Opening (2–3 sentences):** the exact role title, one specific and true reason for this company (product, mission, engineering blog, tech stack, a referral by name), and the claim the letter will prove.
2. **Proof (4–6 sentences):** one story from `master/stories.md` matching the posting's top requirement. Problem, what the person did, result with a number from the master.
3. **Fit (3–4 sentences):** two or three more requirements from the posting, each tied to evidence on the resume. Name one real gap honestly if it matters, and how it is being closed.
4. **Close (1–2 sentences):** interest in a conversation, thanks.

**Sign-off:** "Sincerely," and the full name.

**Rules**

- No repeated resume bullets word for word; expand or connect them.
- No flattery without a fact ("your innovative company"); name the specific thing.
- First person is fine here (unlike the resume).
- Every number and claim traces to `master/` (`check.py` enforces numbers).
