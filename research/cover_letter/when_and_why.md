# Cover letters: when and why

## Findings

- In a field experiment with 7,287 applications to real postings, callback rates were 10.7% with no cover letter, 12.5% with a generic one, and 16.4% with a tailored one: a 53% relative lift for tailored letters over none. [S03, C]
- In the same study's survey of 236 hiring professionals, 87% said they read cover letters and 81% valued tailored letters significantly more than generic ones. [S03, C]
- Google says most of its roles don't require a cover letter, and letters are mainly read "when the team needs more context." [S12, D]
- Employers in the eye-tracking study judged the resume first; the letter does not replace it. [S01, C]

## What the agent does

- **Write a tailored letter whenever the application has a field for one**, when the posting asks for one, or when a referral or recruiter is involved. The evidence for tailored letters is directional but consistent [S03].
- **Generic letters are not produced.** Every letter names the company, the role, and evidence tied to that posting's requirements.
- **Skip it** only when the employer says not to send one, or the person decides to skip it. Note the reason in `match_report.md`.
- **Use the letter for what the resume can't say:** why this company, context for a career switch or gap, a referral, relocation, a story with more detail than a bullet.
- One page, 250–400 words. Structure in `structure.md`. The AI caveat in `ai_and_signal.md` decides how the draft is finished.
