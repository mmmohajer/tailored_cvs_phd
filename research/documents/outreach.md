# Professor email and follow-up

There is no graded study in this folder of cold emails to doctoral supervisors, or of how many follow-ups raise an offer. Treat the shape below as grade D. The position's own instructions win. The agent drafts the email. It does not send it.

## What the agent does

**One professor per position**, the closest real overlap with the person's work. Their name, title, and group come from `positions/<slug>/`. If the page names no person, the email is not written yet: ask who to write to.

**`professor_email.md`**

- Subject line, then the message. 120–180 words. Supervisors at one Australian university described the subject as not the deciding factor, provided it says what the email is. Their examples name the person as a prospective PhD student and name the area. Vague subjects ("Hello", "Enquiry") are the ones they told applicants to avoid. [S25, D]
- The subject names this vacancy or this research area. The gap goes in the body, not in the subject.
- One sentence on who the person is (degree, institution, the project that overlaps). Grades, if named, stay on the original scale. [S25, D]
- One specific paper, problem, or advertised project. If the person has not read it, leave `[you: the paper or problem you actually read]` and do not invent a citation. A paper that is mentioned should be one the professor can find. [S25, D]
- One ask: whether they are considering students, or a short conversation. Not a request to be admitted, and not a claim that a place exists.
- Say the custom CV is attached. Do not attach a different person's CV.
- No second professor in the same email. The same text does not go to a list of supervisors. [S25, D]
- On a supervisor-first route, one university's advisors suggest writing 8–12 weeks before the deadline. [S25, D] An advertised vacancy whose deadline is closer than that is already inside the window: the portal form is the application, and the email does not replace it.

**`follow_up.md`**

A plan the person carries out. Relative dates, anchored to a deadline when the position has one.

1. **Day 0.** Send `professor_email.md` with `cv.pdf`. The cover letter goes to the professor only when the position tells applicants to email a letter. Otherwise it is for the formal application.
2. **If they reply.** Stop this sequence. Answer only what they asked. New facts still have to be true.
3. **If there is no reply after 10–14 days.** One follow-up, three or four sentences, no new claims, no apology paragraph. Include it in the plan as a draft. A science college tells its own students that about a week is enough to wait. [S26, D] Ten to fourteen days is the default here so the reminder is not faster than that advice and is still a single note.
4. **If there is still no reply.** Stop. The exception is a deadline inside the next week: one sentence that names the date, then stop.
5. **Do not** mail several professors in the same group the same text on the same day. Do not follow up more than once unless they asked for something.

The plan names the professor, the send date as "the day you send," and the deadline if one is known. It does not invent a meeting or a reply.
