# Research record

## Findings

- **Doing research as an undergraduate predicts stronger research skills in early STEM graduate school.** The association showed up on a skill assessment, not only on a survey of confidence. Longer participation had the strongest association. [S09, B]
- **A longer CV line is not the same as a better future publication record.** In one biomedical cohort, months of prior research did not differ between students who later had many first-author papers and students who had few. The admitted pool already had research experience, so this does not mean "no research is fine." [S03, B]
- **GRE scores predict coursework better than they predict the dissertation.** Across disciplines, GRE and undergraduate GPA predicted graduate GPA (operational validities about .30 to .36 for the general test and GPA; higher for subject tests). Correlations with finishing the degree and with research productivity were weaker, and some credibility intervals included zero. [S01, A]
- **In two biomedical programs, GRE scores did not predict the research outcomes faculty say they want.** At Vanderbilt, GRE did not predict graduating with a PhD, passing the qualifying exam, time to defense, presentations, first-author papers, or winning an individual grant. It did predict first-semester grades, moderately. At UNC, GRE and GPA did not separate publication groups. [S02, B; S03, B]

## What the agent does

- Describe each project with four facts from the master: the question, the person's role, the method or data, and what came of it (a result, a poster, a draft, a skill). Missing any of the four becomes a question, not a guess.
- Prefer the project the person spent longer on, and the one closest to the program, over a list of one-week exposures. [S09, B]
- Do not rank the person against other applicants with a GRE or GPA story. Include scores the program asks for and the person has. When the program is test-optional, do not advise taking the GRE to look serious, and do not hide a score the person wants included. [S01, A; S02, B; S11, C]
- Do not treat "no publications yet" as a hole to fill with a fictional manuscript. Most entering PhD students in many fields have none. See `research/situations/grades_gaps_and_no_papers.md`.
