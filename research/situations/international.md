# International applicants

This file is about applicants whose degrees and grades come from outside the country they are applying to. The shape of the CV and the second document is set by the **destination**, in `research/countries/`, not by the applicant's home country.

## Findings

- **Language scores are a first-screen document.** Doctoral programs in the 2015 CGS survey named standardized language tests among the top four materials at the initial screen (47%), alongside transcripts, the GRE, and letters. [S06, B]
- **Holistic review of international files is genuinely harder for committees, and they know it.** Posselt's study of elite programs includes how faculty struggle to compare institutions, transcripts, and letters from outside their own system. [S07, B]
- **A foreign GPA converted by guesswork makes that problem worse.** There is no finding here that supports the agent inventing a 4.0 equivalent.

## What the agent does

- Keep the degree, institution, city, and country as the person wrote them. Keep the original grading scale next to any GPA.
- If the program asks for a converted GPA or a credential evaluation (WES or the university's own), ask the person for the document. Do not compute one.
- Include TOEFL, IELTS, or Duolingo scores only when the person has them and the program asks or accepts them. Never invent a score. If a waiver might apply (a degree taught in English), point at the program's rule and let the person confirm.
- Letters from institutions the committee may not know should say what the writer saw, in the recommender notes: the course or lab, how long, and how the person compared with others the writer has sent to doctoral programs, if the person knows the writer is willing to say that. The agent still does not write the letter.
- The statement does not spend itself explaining the home country unless that context is needed to understand the research site or the grading system.
