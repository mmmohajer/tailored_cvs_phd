# Career switcher into tech

Someone with a career in another field moving into a software, data, or IT role.

## Findings

- Employers widely announced dropping degree requirements, but actual hiring of people without a BA into those roles rose by only ~3.5 percentage points; overall, fewer than 1 in 700 hires changed. 45% of firms changed the posting "in name only." [S14, B]
- 88% of employers agree their systems screen out qualified people who don't match the job description's exact criteria. [S05, B]
- 70% of employers say they use skills-based hiring, mostly at interview and screening. [S06, B]
- Clearer writing increased hires by 8%, with the largest gains for weaker writers. [S10, A]

**What this means:** "skills over degrees" is real at some employers and mostly talk at others [S14]. A switcher's resume has to supply direct, checkable evidence of tech skills, in the job description's words [S05], because a past title won't do it.

No controlled study tests resume formats for switchers specifically; the layout guidance below is grade D.

## What the agent does

- **Order:** Header → Summary → Skills → Projects → Experience → Education and Training (see `../sections/order.md`).
- **Summary bridges the two careers in one sentence:** the target role, the strongest tech evidence, and the domain advantage. Example: "Backend developer with three deployed Django projects, bringing six years of logistics operations experience to supply-chain software."
- **Projects carry the tech proof:** 2–4 finished, deployed projects with links and real usage or test evidence. Projects that use the old domain are strongest (a nurse building a clinical scheduling tool).
- **Prior experience, rewritten, not hidden:** keep real titles and dates. Pick bullets that show transferable results: automation built, data analyzed, processes improved, people trained, budgets managed. Translate jargon into plain words.
- **Tech used at the old job counts.** Scripts, SQL reports, spreadsheet automation, internal tools: if it was real and is in the master, it goes in a bullet.
- **Training:** bootcamps, degrees in progress, certificates go under Education and Training with dates.
- **Do not** relabel a non-tech job with a tech title, or present course projects as paid work.
- **Target roles where the old domain is an advantage** (fintech for ex-accountants, healthtech for clinicians); the agent notes this in `match_report.md` when it applies.
