# Returning applicants and industry

## Findings

- **Committees are built to read academic records.** The first screen in the CGS survey was transcripts, tests, letters, and language scores. A decade of employment does not appear on that list. [S06, B]
- **Research skill, not job seniority, is what the STEM evidence talks about.** Prior research participation, especially longer participation, predicted research-skill performance in graduate school. [S09, B] A manager title does not substitute for that.
- **There is no audit study in this folder of "industry applicants get fewer PhD offers."** Do not invent a penalty, and do not invent a bonus for "real-world experience."

## What the agent does

- Keep jobs on the CV with true titles and dates. Rewrite a bullet as research only when the work had a question, a method, and a result the person can point to (an evaluation, a dataset with a finding, an internal study).
- The statement gives the reason for doctoral training in one short paragraph: what they cannot answer in the job, and the preparation that makes the degree the next step. No apology for having worked.
- Letters: at least one writer who can speak to research or to a sustained analytical project. A workplace letter is useful when the program allows it and the writer saw that kind of work. It does not replace a research advisor if one exists. [S03, B]
- Years away from school are dates, not a character flaw. Ask what the person did. Include caregiving, work, or further study only if they want it in the file.
