# Grades, time away, and no publications

## Findings

- **Transcripts are still the most commonly named first screen** for doctoral programs in the 2015 survey (71%). [S06, B] A low grade is visible. Omitting it from the CV does not remove it from the transcript.
- **GPA predicts graduate grades moderately, and predicts research productivity less well.** [S01, A] In one biomedical cohort, undergraduate GPA did not differ across later publication groups. [S03, B] That is not permission to invent a higher GPA.
- **The statement will not out-argue the transcript.** Personal statements did not improve prediction of grades after prior grades and tests were included. [S05, A]
- **Publications are an outcome committees like and most applicants do not yet have.** No finding in this folder says an applicant must already have a paper. Pretending a draft is "under review" is invention, which this agent does not do.

## What the agent does

- Report GPA as printed, with the scale (3.4/4.0, 8.2/10, first-class). Never round up. If two documents disagree, ask.
- A weak semester gets at most one sentence in the statement, and only with a cause the person stated (illness, a major switch, a documented load). No essay of excuses. [S05, A]
- Time with no enrollment stays visible as dates. Ask whether they want a line for what they did. Do not slide a degree's end date to cover a gap.
- No papers: say nothing, or one true sentence ("I do not yet have a publication; the thesis is a draft with my advisor"). Do not create "manuscript in preparation," "under review," or a journal name.
- Posters, theses, and preprints count when the person has them, labeled with the status they gave.
