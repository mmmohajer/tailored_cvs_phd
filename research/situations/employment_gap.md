# Employment gaps

## Findings

- In an audit study of ~3,400 applications to professional jobs, callback rates were 15.2% for continuously employed applicants, 9.3% for unemployed applicants, and 5.1% for parents who had left work to care for children. [S13, A]
- Almost half of surveyed employers screened out resumes with a gap longer than six months, automatically, on that criterion alone. [S05, B]

No controlled study tests *how* to present a gap. The guidance below is grade D, aimed at the two mechanisms the evidence shows: automated gap filters [S05] and human judgments about commitment [S13].

## What the agent does

- **Never hide a gap by bending dates.** Month and year stay accurate. A dates mismatch found in a background check costs more than a gap.
- **Label gaps of 6+ months with one honest line, as its own entry**, so a reader or system sees a reason instead of a hole:
  - `### Career Break — Family Caregiving | Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY`
  - `### Independent Study and Projects | Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY` with 1–2 bullets on real work done (courses completed, projects shipped).
- **Fill with real work done during the gap:** freelance, contract, volunteer, open source, courses. Each needs dates and is in the master.
- **Keep it short.** One line for the reason, no apology or detailed explanation. The cover letter can add one neutral sentence if the person wants.
- The reason is the person's choice to share; the agent asks in `master/open_questions.md` and never assumes (health, caregiving, layoff, immigration, study).
- Short gaps (under ~6 months) need nothing; year-only date formats can cover them only if the whole resume uses year-only dates consistently.
