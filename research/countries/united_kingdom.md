# United Kingdom

The UK doctorate is short, usually three or four years, with limited coursework. Applicants are often expected to know the project, or the supervisor, at the start. Oxford and Cambridge are an elite slice of that system; use them for the pattern, then obey the department's own page. [S14, B]

## Findings

- **Humanities and social sciences assessed the project.** In interviews with 65 staff, most HSS participants described the proposal as the heart of a successful application. Materials were transcripts, a CV, a research proposal, writing samples, and two or three references. Six of seven HSS departments required a master's. A proposal had to be original and feasible, and aligned with a supervisor who had the expertise and the capacity. Excellent applicants were turned away when that fit was missing. Selectors also wanted to see that the applicant could already write: several said the program would not teach writing from nothing. [S14, B]
- **Science, technology, engineering, mathematics, and medicine assessed the person against an existing project.** Those departments asked for transcripts, a CV, a research statement or statement of purpose, and two or three references. A full proposal was not required. Several participants called it unrealistic to expect one. Applicants usually named pre-defined projects or groups. The academic record and technical skills decided whether someone could start immediately; supervisor capacity often decided which project, not whether there was an offer at all. Most successful applicants had a master's even where it was not required. [S14, B]
- **Contact before the form is normal in this system, and uneven.** Applicants often write to supervisors first. Some supervisors only say whether they have room. Others comment on a draft, which changes the proposal the committee later reads. [S14, B]

## What the agent does

The four files are always written: custom CV, cover letter, email to one professor, and a follow-up plan. The proposal or the project statement below is the extra essay. The cover letter does not replace it.

**Humanities and social sciences, or any page that asks for a proposal**

- Write `research_proposal.md`, not a US-style life-of-the-mind statement. Question, why it matters, what has already been said (only works the person knows), method, and why it can be done in three or four years. Feasible and new, both. [S14, B]
- Use the department's word limit. If it is silent, 1,000–1,500 words, and pass `--max-pages 4` so a real proposal is not failed for being longer than a US statement.
- A writing sample is chosen from work the person already wrote. Argument first. The agent does not ghostwrite a chapter.
- The CV is about two pages: degree and class as printed (First, 2:1, or the home scale), master's thesis title, research, languages. No photo. Do not convert a degree class into a 4.0 GPA.
- Name the proposed supervisor only if the page or the person supplies them. If no one with that specialty is in the materials, say the application is not ready. [S14, B]

**STEMM, or an advertised studentship**

- The prose file is `statement.md`: why this project, which skills and courses match it, one proof story. Do not produce a fake independent proposal when the page lists the project already. [S14, B]
- CV page one shows the techniques and the degree record the project needs.
- If several projects may be ranked, the statement addresses the ones the person actually wants, in their order.

**Both**

- References: the number on the page, otherwise two. Writers who can speak to research or to an extended piece of writing.
- Funding is often a second form (a doctoral training partnership or a research-council competition). If that form is in the folder, answer it. Do not assume an international applicant is eligible; the page says who can be funded. Flag the question rather than promising a stipend.
