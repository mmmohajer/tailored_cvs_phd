# Length

## Findings

- **Experienced candidates: two pages is fine, and was preferred.** In a hiring simulation, reviewers picked two-page resumes 2.3x as often as one-page versions of similar candidates: 1.4x for entry level, 2.6x for mid level, 2.9x for managerial roles. Two-page resumes also scored higher for summarizing credentials (8.6 vs 7.1). [S02, C]
- **The second page only gets read if the first earns it.** Eye-tracking showed engaged recruiters spend as much time on page two as page one, but time on page two is strongly predicted by how compelling page one is. Pages after two perform worse regardless. [S01, C]
- **Google's own recruiters:** one page for business and internship roles, no more than two pages for engineering roles. [S12, D]
- **More is not better when it is clutter.** The worst-performing resumes in the eye-tracking study had long sentences, multiple columns, and little white space. [S01, C]

## Reading the evidence together

S02 says length helped, but its two-page versions carried *more real content* than the one-page versions. Nobody has shown that padding helps. The defensible rule is: **length follows real, relevant content, capped by level.**

## What the agent does

| Level | Default | Hard cap |
|---|---|---|
| Internship | 1 page | 1 page |
| Entry / new grad | 1 page | 2 pages only with substantial internships or shipped work |
| Intermediate (2–5 yrs) | 1–2 pages | 2 pages |
| Senior (5–10 yrs) | 2 pages | 2 pages |
| Staff / principal | 2 pages | 2 pages; selected impact goes on page one |
| Cover letter | 1 page, 250–400 words | 1 page |

- Two pages is a ceiling, not a target. If page two would hold only a few lines, cut repetition (for example, Selected Impact lines repeated under Experience) and fit one page. [default, D]
- Never pad to reach a second page. Never shrink below 10pt or squeeze margins under 0.5in to avoid one; cut the weakest bullets instead.
- Page one must carry the strongest evidence for the target job (S01: page two depends on page one).
- The master resume has **no** length limit. Limits apply only to tailored output.
