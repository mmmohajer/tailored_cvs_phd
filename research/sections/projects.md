# Projects

## Findings

- Google's recruiters invite personal and class projects, "more applicable to tech," and ask engineering candidates to link GitHub or prominent open-source work. Projects must be relevant to the job. [S12, D]
- Most employers of new grads look for proof of problem-solving and teamwork, and 70% use skills-based hiring. Projects are the main way candidates without experience show those skills. [S06, B; S07, B]
- Skills claims without proof rarely change who gets hired. [S14, B]

No controlled study compares resumes with and without projects. The guidance below is grade D, consistent with the B-grade findings above.

## What the agent does

**When to include**

- Always for internship, new grad, bootcamp, and career-switch resumes.
- For intermediate and senior people, only when a project shows something the jobs don't (a stack the posting wants, open-source maintainership, a product with real users).
- Staff: open source, talks, or publications instead of side projects, unless the project is significant.

**Format**

`### Project Name | Main stack | [github.com/handle/repo](https://github.com/handle/repo)`

- 1–3 bullets: what it does and for whom, the hardest technical decision, and the evidence it works (users, stars, uptime, test coverage, a deployed URL), all from the master.
- 2–4 projects maximum. Finished and deployed beats many half-done ones.
- No tutorial clones presented as original work. Course projects are labeled as course projects.
- Every link must work. The agent flags links it cannot verify in `match_report.md` for the person to check.
