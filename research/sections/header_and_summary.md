# Header and summary

## Findings

- Top-performing resumes in the eye-tracking study had "an overview or mission statement at the top of the first page." [S01, C]
- Google's recruiters: include contact information and email at the top; objective statements are not needed, and if used they must be relevant to the job. [S12, D]
- Contact details placed in a document header/footer can be skipped by some parsers. [S04, C; S09, C]

## What the agent does

**Header (three lines)**

1. Full name as the person wants to be addressed.
2. Target title plus 2–4 core skills from the job description, for example `Backend Engineer | Python · Django · PostgreSQL`. Use the posting's title when the person's real work matches it.
3. City and region, email, phone, LinkedIn, GitHub or portfolio. Links show as readable text (`github.com/handle`), not "click here."

**Summary (intermediate and up; optional for new grads)**

- 2–3 sentences, no first-person pronouns, no clichés ("results-driven," "passionate team player").
- Sentence 1: level, domain, and years, all true and traceable to the master.
- Sentence 2: the one or two results that best match the job, with numbers.
- Sentence 3 (optional): what the person is targeting, when it is not obvious from the history (career switchers, domain changes).
- Rewritten for every tailored resume. It is the "overview" S01 found at the top of the best resumes.

**Not included:** an "Objective" line, "References available upon request," photos, full street addresses.
