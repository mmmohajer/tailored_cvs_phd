# Section order

## Findings

- Recruiters scan for job titles, then company, dates, and education, top-down on the left side. [S01, C]
- For students, Google's recruiters say education goes first, under contact information, then experience (internships and jobs), then projects, awards, leadership, extracurriculars. [S12, D]
- For engineering roles, Google's recruiters recommend listing programming languages near the top and linking GitHub or open-source work. [S12, D]
- Employers of new grads weigh major, internships, and industry experience most when they don't screen by GPA. [S06, B]

There is no controlled study of section order. Order follows one principle backed by S01: **the strongest evidence for this job goes where the eye lands first.**

## What the agent does

**Internship / new grad / bootcamp with no tech job yet**

1. Header
2. Education (degree, school, graduation date)
3. Skills
4. Experience (internships, co-ops, relevant part-time work)
5. Projects (moves above Experience when projects are stronger than the jobs)
6. Leadership, awards, activities (optional)

**Intermediate and senior**

1. Header
2. Summary (2–3 lines)
3. Skills
4. Experience
5. Projects (only if they add something the jobs don't show)
6. Education
7. Certifications, publications, talks (optional)

**Staff / principal**

1. Header
2. Summary (scope and kind of staff work)
3. Selected impact (optional, 3–4 lines of org-level results)
4. Experience
5. Skills
6. Talks, publications, open source (optional)
7. Education

**Career switcher**

1. Header
2. Summary (the target role and the bridge)
3. Skills
4. Projects (tech evidence first)
5. Experience (prior career, rewritten for transferable results)
6. Education and training

Within a section: reverse chronological order. Section names stay standard (see `../format/ats_and_parsing.md`).
