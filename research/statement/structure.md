# Statement structure

## Findings

- **Background plus reasons for applying are the stable moves** in a small study of successful statements. Fields emphasize different parts. [S10, C]
- **A working faculty checklist is qualifications, focus, and fit.** [S13, D]
- **Committees read the statement to distinguish people, not to re-score the GPA.** [S05, A; S06, B]
- **There is no experiment that assigns this paragraph order and measures offers.** The shape below is a house default (grade D) that forces the three things readers say they look for.

## What the agent does

Use the program's prompt if it has one (some ask for a research proposal, some for a personal history, some for both). Otherwise five moves, 700–1,000 words, under two pages:

1. **Question (4–6 sentences).** The problem the person wants to work on, specific enough that a faculty member in the area recognizes it. No "I have always been passionate about."
2. **Preparation (two or three short paragraphs).** One project in enough detail to show how they work, then a second piece of evidence if it adds a method. Numbers and titles from the master.
3. **Fit (one paragraph).** Why this department: two or three faculty or a center, each with a real overlap. Placeholders if the person has not read the work. See `research/documents/fit.md`.
4. **Gap, only if needed (one or two sentences).** A true missing piece and what they have already done about it.
5. **Close (two or three sentences).** What they want to learn in the program, tied to the question in the opening. No job-application close ("I would welcome the chance to interview").

Header: the same three lines as the CV, then the date, then "Admissions Committee," the program, and the university. Salutation: "Dear Admissions Committee," unless the program names a person.

Sign-off: "Sincerely," and the full name.
