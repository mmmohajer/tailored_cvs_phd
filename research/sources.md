## Notes on bias and limits

- S01 predicts grades, faculty ratings, and exam scores better than it predicts finishing the degree or publishing. A test that forecasts coursework is not the same as a test that forecasts a dissertation.
- S02 and S03 only include students who were admitted and enrolled. They cannot say whether GRE or GPA sorted the applicants who were rejected. Both are biomedical programs.
- S04 and S05 average across many kinds of programs, including non-doctoral ones. "Weak predictor of grades" does not mean committees ignore the document. S06 and S07 are about what committees actually do.
- S06 is from 2015. S11, a decade later, finds most responding graduate units no longer require a test. Use S06 for the shift from numbers early to letters and statements late. Use the program's current page for whether a GRE is required this year.
- S07 and S08 are elite, highly selective programs. Less selective departments may read more of every file and weigh pedigree less.
- S11 comes from the company that sells the GRE, with a 17% partial response rate. Use the direction (tests are often optional now); treat the exact percentages loosely.
- S10 describes statements that succeeded in a small set of programs. It is a map of the genre, not proof that this shape causes admission.
- S01–S13 are mostly US evidence. They are hard rules for US applications and only analogies elsewhere. S14 is Oxford and Cambridge, not every UK department. S15 is four biomedical schools. S16 and S17 are official instructions, so they bind applications to those calls and do not describe every department in the country.
- S18–S28 are instructions and career-service notes. None of them is an experiment on whether a two-page CV beats a one-page CV. When they disagree, the saved position page wins, then the country file, then the early-career range in `format/cv_structure.md`. S23 is about jobs in Germany, not about a Graduiertenkolleg. S28 is about academic posts, not about a first PhD application. S27 is one Padua curriculum.
