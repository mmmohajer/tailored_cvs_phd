Dear Admissions Committee,

I am applying for the PhD in Computer Science. I want to work on programming tools that show a student why a program fails to type-check, and the Hale group's teaching language is the work I can bring to that problem.

For my master's thesis I built a small language whose stepper prints each type-checking rule as it fires. Dr. Jonah Hale supervised the project. I chose the rules, implemented the stepper in OCaml, and used it with students who were meeting type inference for the first time. The workshop paper with Dr. Hale describes that stepper. What I still cannot answer is how much of the error a student can repair when the stepper shows the rule and nothing else. A doctoral project is the place to study that, because the thesis stopped at building the tool.

Harbor's programming-languages group is where I want to ask it. The department trains students to build languages, and my preparation is that thesis, the OCaml implementation, and the paper. I have not studied learning outcomes in a controlled class, and I am not claiming that the stepper already teaches better than an ordinary compiler error.

I am asking to be considered for the doctoral program. I would like to continue this line of work with a supervisor in programming languages. My CV is enclosed.
